Binary numbers and bitwise operators

Bitwise operators work on the binary representation of integers. Visualise the bits in aligned positions before applying AND, OR, XOR, NOT or shifts.

Real-life example

Imagine two rows of tiny switches. Each switch is either OFF (0) or ON (1). A bitwise operator compares switches in matching positions.

Step-by-step explanation

  1. 5 is 0101 and 3 is 0011 when written using four bits.
  2. AND keeps a position only when both bits are 1: 0001 = 1.
  3. OR keeps a position when either bit is 1: 0111 = 7. XOR keeps different bits: 0110 = 6.

Python example

a = 5       # binary: 0101
b = 3       # binary: 0011
print(a & b)
print(a | b)
print(a ^ b)

Expected output

1
7
6

Common mistake

Do not confuse and/or (Boolean logic) with &/| (bitwise operations). They are different operators.

Remember this

AND = both; OR = at least one; XOR = different. Align bits before calculating.
