Trimming and replacing text

Cleaning text often means removing unwanted characters from its ends or replacing selected text. strip() works at the ends; it does not remove spaces from the middle.

Real-life example

Imagine a label with extra spaces around its edges and a typo in the middle. strip() cleans the edges; replace() swaps a chosen piece of text.

Step-by-step explanation

  1. strip() removes whitespace from the beginning and end.
  2. replace() creates a new string with the requested text replaced.
  3. The original raw string remains unchanged.

Python example

raw = "  hello world  "
clean = raw.strip()
fixed = clean.replace("world", "Python")
print(clean)
print(fixed)

Expected output

hello world
hello Python

Common mistake

strip() does not remove spaces in the middle of a sentence. Use replace() or split/join if you need to normalise inner spaces.

Remember this

strip the edges; replace the exact text you name.
