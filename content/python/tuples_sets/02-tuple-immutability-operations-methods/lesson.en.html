Tuple immutability, operations and methods

Tuples are immutable, but they can contain references to mutable objects such as lists. Concatenation, repetition, comparison, count() and index() are useful tuple operations.

Real-life example

A tuple is a sealed record: you can inspect it and make a new tuple from it, but you cannot replace one of its stored positions.

Step-by-step explanation

  1. count(4) reports one occurrence.
  2. index(7) reports position 1.
  3. The + operator creates a new tuple; the original stays (4, 7).

Python example

point = (4, 7)
print(point.count(4))
print(point.index(7))
new_point = point + (9,)
print(new_point)

Expected output

1
1
(4, 7, 9)

Common mistake

Immutability prevents replacing tuple slots, but a tuple can contain a mutable list whose contents may change.

Remember this

A tuple cannot change its item references, but a mutable object inside it may still change.
